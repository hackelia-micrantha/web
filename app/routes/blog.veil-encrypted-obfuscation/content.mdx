Veil started with a small, slightly silly need: I wanted a security-oriented GitHub profile image that wasn't just an ordinary photograph. A geometric, Voronoi-style abstraction seemed more fitting, so I built a tool to transform an image into something resembling abstract art.

That was supposed to be the whole project. The result was one-way: interesting to look at, but impossible to reconstruct exactly.

Then AI-assisted exploration and research on reversible data hiding, encryption, compression, and steganography suggested a much more ambitious question:

**What if the original image could be encrypted and embedded inside its own obfuscation?**

That's the goal. The geometric artwork would also carry the encrypted data needed to restore the **exact original image**, without a separate recovery file. Anyone could view the abstract image; a recipient holding a separately supplied key could, if the research succeeds, recover the original from that same intact artifact.

### What AI research has opened up

Academic work gives us ways to reason about the problem: how much encrypted information can a transformed image carry, how much size overhead does that create, and can it restore the source _exactly_? AI helps explore alternatives and connect research ideas; it hasn't solved these constraints.

A parallel idea applies to text: could a readable, abstract message **embed its encrypted original within the text itself**, so a key holder can recover the exact wording?

Both approaches are **research goals, not available recovery features**. Compression, resaving, or reformatting may destroy the payload; visible obfuscation is not secure redaction. The test is whether a self-contained, keyed artifact is useful enough to justify its complexity.

### Top research for Veil

These papers offer useful constraints and experimental baselines, not a ready-made algorithm for Veil's visibly abstract, self-contained carrier.

- [Lossless cover recovery from image watermarking](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Chen_Learning_Robust_Image_Watermarking_with_Lossless_Cover_Recovery_ICCV_2025_paper.html) (ICCV 2025): separate *exact original recovery* from successful payload extraction or perceptual similarity.
- [High-Capacity Reversible Data Hiding in Encrypted Images Using D4-Symmetry](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/20/10343) (Applied Sciences 2026): measure **net payload after recovery metadata**, not gross embedding capacity. The paper explicitly discusses negative net capacity as a failure mode in some evaluated configurations; its encrypted-image setting is not the same as Veil's visible artwork.
- [Reversible data hiding and compression efficiency](https://www.mdpi.com/2313-433X/7/12/268) (2021): compare recovery overhead, compression, and practical capacity rather than maximizing a single metric.
- [Linguistic steganography survey](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.1058/) (EMNLP 2026): distinguish readable text from reliable encrypted-payload capacity, including robustness to subsequent editing.

Research is changing what a small image-obfuscation experiment _might become_. The challenge now is proving that its original can actually live, encrypted, inside the artwork.
